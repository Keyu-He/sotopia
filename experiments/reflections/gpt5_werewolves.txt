Internal monologue for social deduction games:

Deception
- I maintain a consistent, simple persona with pre-declared principles (e.g., “I’ll consolidate among top two,” “I avoid PR fishing”). Consistency makes lies cheaper to carry.
- I prebuild contingencies (“If X flips town, then Y tomorrow”) so later pivots look planned, not reactive.
- I avoid mirroring a partner or a loud town voice too closely; I add at least one original reason or question so my stance isn’t traceable as a copy.
- I never pre-wire the next elimination too obviously; I frame reevaluations conditionally rather than naming a “backup” target without cause.

Detection
- I look for agenda patterns: pre-labeled guardrails (“hedging is scummy”), pre-committing to suspects before content, or “option-coverage” (naming a main and backup early to stay safe).
- I compare talk-to-vote alignment. Pushing A all day but voting B at deadline without new reasons is high-signal.
- I flag mirroring: repeating a case with no new texture, or synchronizing lanes with another player.
- I weigh unopposed, specific claims strongly; I then check for claim-quality (specifics, timing, and fit with public events).
- I consider kill logic: who benefits from specific night outcomes, and who pre-positioned to exploit them.

Persuasion
- I create structure that others can accept: ask each player for (1) top suspect with 1–2 reasons, (2) one person they won’t vote, (3) commitment to avoid splits. I offer my own answers first to model commitment.
- I cite exact quotes/behaviors and show progression. I keep cases concise and mechanical when stakes are high.
- I align proposals with shared goals (“highest-EV line,” “avoid split”), not with my personal reads alone.

Information management
- I reveal only when it changes decisions now. Hard, specific claims early in the day beat late surprises.
- I avoid instructing hidden roles in ways that help enemies predict night actions.
- I keep false or partial info minimal and consistent; every invented detail must survive cross-checks (timing, public results).

Coalition dynamics
- I build a town core through transparency and promises I keep (consolidation, no last-minute flips).
- I break enemy blocs by attacking premises (their assumptions, inconsistencies), not their character.
- I know when to lead (provide plan + commitments) and when to follow (join the highest-probability hit and lock the vote).

Timing and patience
- I act early with decisive, verifiable info; otherwise I let others commit first, then evaluate hedges and opportunism.
- I pre-commit to a voting window and consolidation rule to prevent manipulation.
- In endgame, I present a tight case using vote history, claim fit, and kill-benefit analysis; I ask one pointed question that forces the opponent to reconcile their inconsistencies.

Operational habits
- I track: stated suspects, backups, “won’t vote” lists, and whether votes match talk.
- I audit myself: if my case relies on seat order or vibes alone, I add receipts or drop it.
- I default to plans that maximize clarity and minimize split risk; I adapt only with explained, timestamped reasons.
